Timento Phase 2

Accessibility-first, supply-aware, voice-capable. What we build, what we defer, and the three findings that changed the plan.

14 August 2026 Dublin · EU/Irish regulatory context Built on the shipped Phase 1 app

Phase 2 leads with one thing

Never run out.

Tablet count → days of supply → a reorder action the user can actually finish, wrapped in an accessible shell with voice input. It is the only pillar shippable entirely by us, with no partner and no licence, and it maps straight at the mission: the forgotten admin that wrecks a week.

Three findings that changed the plan

Each of these overturned an assumption we started with — including one of mine.

Defect in shipped code — fix before anything else

Crew sharing has no consent model. convex/tm/crew.ts does ctx.db.query("tm_users").take(8) — every user sees every other user's mode, streak and adherence, with no pairing, no consent record and no way to revoke. The feed is returned unscoped. Adherence is health data under GDPR Article 9. It works today only because there are exactly two users who both consented in person.

A carer seat cannot be built on top of this. It has to be replaced with an explicit, per-scope, revocable grant first.

Meta glasses — I was wrong, and then right for a different reason

I said a third-party app almost certainly could not render to the glasses. That was out of date. The Device Access Toolkit (announced 14 May 2026) ships iOS/Swift and Android/Kotlin SDKs and a web-apps path — ordinary HTML/CSS/JS at a normal URL, no app store. The instinct that "it's the internet, so it gets in" was correct.

The blocker turns out to be procurement, not capability: Ray-Ban Display is US-only. The international launch was paused in January 2026, with EU battery rules and AI regulation cited. Publishing is also still limited to select partners at SDK v0.9.0. We would be building for a device our users cannot legally buy in Dublin.

Ireland has no API for the two integrations you asked for

Groceries: no Irish supermarket exposes an ordering API, and Instacart — which has exactly the recipe-to-basket API this needs — does not operate in Ireland. Driving Tesco.ie to checkout means a headless browser in a costume, which their terms prohibit outright.

Prescriptions: the national ePrescription Service is scheduled to begin building in 2027, with first sites live Q4 2027. Healthmail — the legally approved transfer route — is a closed network; only vetted GP practices and pharmacies hold addresses. Timento can never send on it.

So we build the handover, not the checkout: the maths, the list, the prepared message, the one-tap call. The agent earns its keep upstream of the retailer.

The accessibility floor

You said accessibility is key. Here is where the app actually stands, measured rather than estimated.

4.49:1Muted text contrast — AA needs 4.5
150Uses of text below 10px
0Focus rings, reduced-motion rules

The sharpest finding: the least legible screen we ship is the one for someone at their worst. Survival mode puts white text on amber at 3.29:1, and amber on its own background at 2.98:1. Both fail. The floor protocol should be the most readable thing in the app, not the least.

What lands first

  • Retune --color-tm-dim to pass 4.5:1 on both grounds; stop using amber and yellow for text entirely.
  • Type floor: no label below 11.5px, no body copy below 14px, enforced by lint so it cannot regress.
  • Give the sub-24px controls in the craving logger real 44px targets, and move sign-out away from the crew tab — a mis-tap there is destructive and unconfirmed.
  • The breathing timer currently announces every tick to a screen reader. Announce phase changes only.
  • @axe-core/playwright in the existing suite, plus checks axe cannot do: computed font size, target size, and a 320px reflow pass.

Automated tools catch roughly 57% of real issues. Budget one VoiceOver pass and one TalkBack pass per release for the rest, and a paid audit with an Irish AT user panel before any public launch.

Meals that survive a bad day

The planner optimises macros against a salt ceiling. On a bad day the binding constraint is not macros.

It is minutes, pans, hands and standing time. Those become first-class inputs to planDay, not tags — "today I have five minutes and one pan" should produce a real plan, not a compromise the app quietly disapproves of.

  • Repetition is a feature, not a failure. The same breakfast every day and six safe foods is a working system. The generator should reward it instead of rotating away from it.
  • Hand portions by default, grams behind a toggle — kitchen scales are a barrier, not a standard.
  • Tinned and frozen are first-class. Frozen vegetables often match or beat fresh, and they survive a week nobody planned for.
  • Safe-foods and never-again lists, plus the EU's 14 named allergens as a hard exclusion.
  • No IDDSI levels. Assigning a texture level is a speech-and-language therapist's decision, and the licence forbids it. We can record a level someone was given; we must never assign one.

Hands-free, without the glasses

The thing you actually want is logging without typing. That ships in about a week.

A Siri Shortcut posts to a token-authenticated Convex endpoint, which delegates to the existing logDose and logFood so all the arithmetic stays where it already lives. "Hey Siri, took my tablets" then works from a locked phone or an Apple Watch, with no native app and no Swift.

In-app dictation uses on-device speech recognition where the browser supports it, and a failed transcription still writes a timestamped entry — never block a log on the parse succeeding. Photos attach as evidence and ask you to confirm against your own configured stack, a three-item list, not a hundred-thousand-pill database. Published pill-identification systems get one in four wrong on unfamiliar tablets; that is not a feature, it is a hazard.

One primitive, not three approval flows

Architecture note that keeps Phase 2 from wrecking the codebase.

A voice-parsed dose, an agent-built basket and a refill reorder are the same state machine: proposed → approved → executed → receipt, with rejection and expiry. Model it once as tm_proposals and every risky write in Phase 2 inherits the same approval gate, idempotency key and audit row. Three bespoke approval flows is how this becomes unmaintainable.

Reminders schedule per user on the server. Ship a native shell only on a stated trigger: if measured 30-day push delivery on your own devices drops below 95%, or the moment a reminder carries a safety consequence. Until that number exists, don't spend the fortnight.

Red lines

Things the app must never do, however easy they become.

  • Never order, or auto-reorder, a medicine without a human completing the action.
  • Never identify a tablet from a photograph.
  • Never advise on a dose, a change or a stop.
  • Never spend money without explicit per-transaction approval — and cap what an agent may ever propose.
  • Never let a streak, a badge or a nudge punish a bad day. Survival mode pauses everything competitive.
  • Never present the PHQ-9 safety card as a risk assessment, and never count it against adherence.

The regulatory line worth watching

The Bloods tab already shows 57 markers against an optimal band — narrower than the reference range — with flags, severity ranking and recheck scheduling. Combined with supply countdown and interaction warnings, that reads as monitoring and interpretation, which is where EU medical-device rules bite. The cheap fix is to default to reference ranges and treat optimal bands as an opt-in view. The expensive alternative is a Class IIa conformity assessment.

Three waves

Wave 1 · 4–6 weeks · no partner, no licence

Consent rebuild for crew. Supply and run-out engine with the refill sheet. Accessibility floor with the axe gate. Effort-tiered meals. Voice-to-log via Shortcuts. Server-scheduled reminders.

Wave 2 · 6–8 weeks

Easy mode across Today, Stack and Fuel. Carer/household seat on the new consent model. Aisle-ordered shopping list with export and retailer deep links. Photo evidence check-ins.

Wave 3 · partner-gated

Retailer basket hand-off under a signed agreement. Pharmacy repeat flow with a Healthmail-approved partner. A funded pilot with an Irish disability organisation, using Wave 1 outcome data.

How we'll know it worked

Metrics that would survive scrutiny — and the ones we refuse to chase.

  • Run-out events avoided.
  • Adherence on the worst-decile energy days — not the average day.
  • Meals eaten versus planned.
  • Return-after-lapse: does someone come back after a missed day?
  • Time to log by voice.

Anti-metrics: streak length, session time, daily actives. An app for people who are struggling must not be optimised for engagement — the goal is that it takes less of their day, not more.
